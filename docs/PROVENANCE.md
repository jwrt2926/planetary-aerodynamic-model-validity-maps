# Data provenance, replication and limitations

- The reported results use processed reference atmospheric profiles for Earth, Mars and Titan, derived from NASA GRAM Suite 2.1.1. The processed CSVs each contain 2,201 rows at 1 km intervals over 0–2,200 km. Their source-file hashes have been checked against the input archives and are recorded in `SHA256SUMS.txt`.
- Earth and Mars reference atmospheres are weighted combinations of 48 sampled geographical/seasonal states each; Titan uses a nominal model profile. The sample is not an established globally converged climatology, and its dispersion is not a formal uncertainty bound.
- Nine plotting scripts correspond to the nine manuscript figures. The final Fig. 9 presentation is regenerated from saved individual-case crossing results rather than from a new GRAM execution. Some figure and analysis scripts were refined following sensitivity and model-consistency reviews; the repository preserves the implementations used for the final figures.
- The 96 individual Earth/Mars continuum boundary values and sampling weights are retained in `data/sampling/`. The saved cases permit replication of the corresponding figure and descriptive statistics without rerunning GRAM. Regenerating atmospheric states underlying these cases requires NASA GRAM and its external datasets.
- The included atmospheric processing scripts are provided to document the sampled-GRAM and Chapman–Enskog/Wilke workflows. GRAM executables and large auxiliary MERRA files are distributed separately by NASA and are not included.
- Model-selection thresholds and mean-free-path conventions are prescribed engineering assumptions, not experimentally identified universal transitions. The classification does not resolve vehicle geometry, shocks, thermochemical nonequilibrium or turbulent closures.
- The discrepancy between GRAM-reported and composition-derived Martian gas constants above roughly 174 km remains unresolved. The primary Martian comparison maps are restricted to 0–100 km.
- The automated tests establish reproducibility of the classification calculations using archived processed inputs. They do not independently validate NASA atmospheric data, actual flight trajectory regimes or geometry-dependent CFD/DSMC predictions.
- Public reuse rights for the underlying data should not be inferred merely from their inclusion in this repository. Refer to NASA's applicable distribution terms for GRAM and associated datasets.
